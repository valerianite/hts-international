Brand foundation

Who HTS is

The business

A family-owned Australian business run by the Hildebrands. HTS sources, buys, services and maintains plastic injection moulding machines and related equipment for manufacturers across Australia, and also supplies material handling, automation, safety and spare-parts products.

Machines and equipment are imported from China, Korea, Japan and Italy. Current supplier brands: Borche, Wensui, CRG, Anhyup, HALS and Maco.

Based in Dandenong South, Victoria.

Who it serves

Plastics manufacturers: automotive, medical, food and beverage, and others, from plant managers to the people who run the machines.

What sets HTS apart

  • Experience. One of the few remaining leading experts in the field in Australia
  • Whole-factory help. Machines, feeding systems, automation, relocation and set-up
  • Personal and hands-on. Family-run, accountable, easy to reach
  • Service after the sale. Diagnostics, repairs, spares and upgrades

The "40+ years" story (confirmed)

HTS itself has traded since the 1990s. Bernhard (Bernie) Hildebrand has worked in the plastics industry since the 1970s and 80s, and that is where the "40+ years" comes from. Copy should say "40+ years of industry experience", never that the company was founded 40 years ago.

Sources: HTS Logo Redesign Project (2022) and www.hts.international3

Clear space

Keep a margin of X on every side of the logo, where X is the height of the letter H in the mark. Nothing (text, image edges, other logos) enters this space.

Minimum size

  • Screen: 80 px wide
  • Print: 20 mm wide

The current website header shows the logo at about 85 px, so this is consistent with existing use.

Master files

HTS-Final/HTS_logo-Final.ai, with PNG exports in colour, black, white and large colour. The 1200 px PNG is the largest raster available; an SVG should be exported from the .ai file for the website.

Typography

Typefaces Approved

HEADINGS
Barlow Condensed
ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ 0123456789

SemiBold and Bold, usually uppercase for headings and buttons. Narrow, upright and squared-off, in keeping with the angular H, T and S in the logo.

BODY
Barlow
ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz 0123456789

Regular for body copy, Medium or SemiBold for emphasis. Highly legible at small sizes, which matters for technical specifications.

Why these: the current site uses a very light weight of DIN Next in light grey, which is hard to read. Barlow keeps a similar industrial, DIN-like character but has stronger weights. Both fonts are free and open source (SIL Open Font License). If they are not in Wix's built-in font list they can be uploaded as custom fonts. Fallback: Arial.

Imagery and partner brands

Photography and supplier logos Proposed

Photography

  • Product: clean side-on shots on white or light grey, consistently sized and lit (the Borche and HALS photography already does this well)
  • Client sites are off limits: HTS installs and customer factories are private and must not be photographed or shown on the website. The market is highly competitive
  • Use instead: supplier product photography, technical diagrams and drawings, and clean product-on-white shots
  • Avoid generic stock imagery, heavy filters and busy backgrounds behind logos
  • The vehicle photos on page 15 are brand reference only, not for the website
  • Place the HTS logo, not a supplier's, in the site header

Supplier and partner logos

Supplier logos arrive in every colour, shape and size. On the current site this makes the Products page look uneven, and the HTS logo is used as a stand-in where no product image exists. Proposed rules:

BORCHE
WENSUI
CRG
  • Show each supplier logo in a same-size white tile with a thin line border
  • Keep supplier logos in their own colours on product pages, in grey on overview grids if permitted by the supplier
  • Never use the HTS logo as a product image; use a product photo or a neutral icon
  • Confirm each supplier's logo-use terms with them

Voice and tone

Write like a skilled tradesperson Proposed

Do

  • Say what the product does and who it is for
  • Use short sentences and plain words
  • Use "we" and "you"
  • Lead with the benefit, then the spec

Don't

  • Use hype words such as "elevate", "unlock" or "cutting-edge"
  • Write long paragraphs without headings
  • Leave typos or run-together words in technical copy
  • Over-promise

Example rewrite (from the current Technology & Upgrades copy)

NOW

"Unlock the future of smarter and more energy-efficient production by upgrading your existing equipment with cutting-edge technology."

PROPOSED

"Upgrade the machines you already have. We help you run them more efficiently and use less energy."

Rewrites keep the original claims only. Any new claims (savings, response times, numbers) must come from the client.

  • The vehicle lettering uses a wide, extended capital typeface, not Barlow. Keep the signwriter's existing artwork as the master for vehicles
  • Colours look warmer in these photos because of sunlight. Ask the signwriter to match the vinyl to red #CD2127 and charcoal #3F4142
